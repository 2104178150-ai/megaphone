# X (Twitter) and Bluesky

> Last checked: 2026-10-15 against X's [Authenticity policy](https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/authenticity)
> (the old platform-manipulation-and-spam URL now redirects there; it covers spam,
> hashtags and engagement) and Bluesky's [Community Guidelines](https://bsky.social/about/support/community-guidelines).
> Neither platform documents how its feeds rank posts, so everything under Format and
> Timing is heuristic.

## Fit

Developers who already follow you, people in your niche, and the accounts that share
dev tools. Reach depends on your network, so a good post *plus* a reply from a relevant
account beats a long thread from a cold account (heuristic).

## Rules (from the platforms)

- **X** (Authenticity policy) doesn't allow, among other things:
  - bulk or aggressive unsolicited replies, mentions or DMs;
  - using trending or popular hashtags to drive attention to your product, or posting
    "excessive, unrelated hashtags";
  - repeatedly posting links without commentary, or replying to unrelated posts to
    promote yours;
  - posting and deleting the same content repeatedly, or near-identical posts;
  - coordinating to exchange likes, reposts or follows, or buying them;
  - running several accounts that post the same content (localised translations are
    allowed).
- **Bluesky**: "Do not send spam or repeatedly post content in ways that disrupt normal
  conversations", and do not "artificially manipulate features or social signals to gain
  unearned reach", including engagement metrics and follower counts.

## Format (heuristics)

- **Lead with a video or GIF** of the aha moment (10–30 seconds, no intro). A visual is
  what stops the scroll.
- **First line** = the outcome, in plain words.
- **The link**: it's often claimed that posts with external links reach fewer people, so
  some people put the link in a reply. That's unverified and changes; for a one-shot
  launch thread, keep the link in the thread (post 4 of the template) where readers will
  find it, and don't spend launch day A/B testing.
- **Threads**: 3–6 posts. 1) demo + outcome, 2) why you built it, 3) how it works,
  4) how to try it + link, 5) what's next / ask for feedback.
- Mention tools or libraries you genuinely built on, once, where it's relevant; their
  maintainers sometimes reshare. Mass-mentioning is against X's rules.
- Hashtags: none or one or two relevant ones. X's rules forbid excessive or unrelated
  hashtags and riding trending ones to promote a product.

## Timing (heuristic)

No official guidance and no reliable public data. Post when the people who follow you
are awake and when you can reply for the next hour or two. For a mostly US + Europe
developer audience, a weekday around 14:00–17:00 UTC (US morning, European afternoon;
22:00–01:00 Beijing) is a common choice; for an audience in your own region, your own
daytime. Two patterns work with the rest of the plan: post the thread the day *before*
your biggest channel (so the demo video is already hosted and shareable), or the same
day, *after* that post, while you're online anyway.

## Build in public

Posting progress (screenshots, numbers, mistakes) in the weeks before launch builds
the audience that shows up on launch day. The launch post can then quote-post your
first progress post.

## Template

```
1/ <Name> turns <input> into <output> in <time>. Open source. 👇
[demo video]

2/ I built it after <specific moment — the author's own words>. <Alternative> needed <pain>.

3/ How it works: <interesting technical bit>.

4/ Try it: <command or link>
   Repo: <link>

5/ It doesn't do <X> yet. What should I build next?
```

## Pitfalls

- A thread with no visual → scrolled past.
- Tagging big accounts asking for reposts → looks like spam, and bulk mentions break
  X's rules.
- The same post from several of your accounts → against X's rules (translations excepted).
- Posting once and disappearing → reply to every quote and reply for the first hours.
